# Product job and observed alternatives

The first session is a complete ready adventure: a host creates a free recoverable account, chooses an episode, shares a room invitation with two real devices on opposite crews, starts, and plays all six interdependent stations. The hosted service supplies access-separated role cards, concurrent controls, real derived machine state, authoritative progression, pause and reconnect. A notebook or spreadsheet can describe a puzzle but does not provide this coordinated network behavior.

The six original stories are The Midnight Lift, The Train That Missed Tomorrow, The Bakery Below the Tide, The Clockwork Orchard, The Borrowed Moon and Post for the Glasshouse. They belong to one product. Later episodes vary maps, crossed circuits, symbolic instructions, weight equations and endings while retaining learnable controls. The first is the most direct onboarding episode; subsequent cards introduce small deductions. Alternative valid cradle balances are accepted.

Primary competitor research found established event and team-adventure products. [Goosechase supports browser/guest/team joining](https://goosechase.com/how-it-works/join-experience), so browser access is not claimed as unique. Its [official pricing article](https://support.goosechase.com/en/articles/4571290-how-much-does-a-goosechase-experience-cost) listed free three-participant experiences and paid single-event tiers when consulted. [Actionbound](https://en.actionbound.com/faq) supports prepared mobile experiences and offline loading, with [personal-use and commercial pricing distinctions](https://en.actionbound.com/pricing). [Loquiz](https://loquiz.com/pricing/) offers a mature event-game platform and logic authoring. These facts do not establish demand for Interlock.

The chosen focus is ready two-crew interdependence instead of requiring the organizer to author a scavenger hunt first. An invitation names an actual room and fixed role; the recipients' actions are needed to move that room forward. Return-use hypotheses are another episode with the same friends and a host inviting a different group. A future capacity subscription is tied to actual larger hosting allowances, not a promise of improved scores or audience growth.

Demand, willingness to pay, retention, acquisition cost and advertising approval are unproven. There are no fabricated customers, player activity, testimonials or revenue claims. A meaningful first-use measure would be two distinct players joining, starting and completing a room; a return measure would be an actual host opening a later room. This release does not report those as established market metrics.
